What the number means

Levels, as locals read them

A rule of thumb from the outfitters and the tubing regulars, not a rule. Cubic feet per second (cfs) is the gauge’s unit. Children twelve and under must wear a life jacket on the river in Lyons; everyone should when it is fast.

Under 40Low. Too shallow for tubes and you will walk the riffles; fine for wading, fishing and sitting on the whitewater-park rocks. Most of September to April.
40 – 100Mellow. Easy tubing through LaVern M. Johnson Park with a life jacket on the kids. Late summer.
100 – 300Prime. The classic float from the Apple Valley Road bridge down through town, with the park’s drops at their best. Usually July into August.
300 – 700Fast. Pushy water and real holes in the park; life jackets, experienced floaters, no first-timers, no small children. Late June and early July in a big snow year.
700 – 1,200High. Not for tubes. Kayakers and rafters only, and the park features wash out. Peak runoff, late May to mid-June.
Over 1,200Flood watch. The Town posts advisories at this level and localized flooding begins; stay off the water and away from cut banks.

Sources for the thresholds: the local tubing outfitters’ guidance and the Town’s 2023 flood-advisory threshold. The Town’s river page ↗ carries the current rules and any closure.

Tubing

Three put-ins, all free. The Apple Valley Road bridge on US 36 just west of town gives the long float through the canyon meadows to LaVern M. Johnson Park. The park itself, on Colorado 7 at the west edge of town, is the short run through the whitewater features. The take-out most people use is at the bottom of the park; a longer float continues under Main Street to the east end. Tubes and life jackets rent in town in summer.

The whitewater park

A quarter-mile of engineered pools and drops on the North St. Vrain inside LaVern M. Johnson Park, rebuilt after the 2013 flood. Kayak play at 300 cfs and up; tubes and bodies at anything less. The bank is sandstone slab and lawn, so it is also the best picnic and spectator spot in town. A Town parking pass applies to the lot on busy days.

Fishing

Wild browns and stocked rainbows on both forks, best in the low, clear water of late summer and fall. Bohn Park has an accessible fishing platform. Up County Road 80, Ralph Price Reservoir in Button Rock Preserve holds browns, rainbows and splake, and needs a City of Longmont permit on top of the Colorado licence: 600 a year, sold from April. Before you go

Closures, weather and the rules that bite

Closures
The county closes trails after storms, for prescribed burns on the Nelson Loop between January and April, and for elk and raptors in season. County trail closures ↗
Mud
Riding or walking a muddy trail ruts it for the season; the county asks you to turn back if you are leaving prints. Rabbit Mountain dries first, Hall Ranch by late spring.
Weather
Afternoon lightning most summer days from about one o’clock; start early. Chinook winds through the canyon mouth in winter. The National Weather Service forecast for Lyons ↗
Fire
Stage 1 and 2 restrictions are common from June; check before any flame, including at the campground. Current fire restrictions ↗
Parking passes
The Town’s park lots use a parking pass on busy days; the county trailheads are free. Parking, on the visitor page →
Emergencies
Call 911. Lyons Fire runs swiftwater rescue on the St. Vrain; Boulder County Sheriff covers the open space.
