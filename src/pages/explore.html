01

Main Street and the stone town

Main Street is US 36 for six blocks, running east–west between the two highway junctions with the hogback closing the view at the west end. The buildings are the point: fifteen structures of locally quarried red sandstone, built between the 1870s and 1917, are listed together on the National Register of Historic Places, and most are still in everyday use as shops, kitchens and offices.

Walk it end to end in twenty minutes. High Street, one block north, has a second small cluster of coffee, wine and the museum. The Town's downtown is an accredited Main Street America program and a state-certified Creative District.

Getting there
Street parking along Main and the side streets, free; a Town lot at 4th and Broadway beside Sandstone Park.
Best for
Coffee, lunch, the galleries and gift shops, and the building fronts themselves. Pick up the walking-tour leaflet at the visitor center in Sandstone Park.

02

The river and the parks

The North and South St. Vrain come out of their canyons and join in the middle of town, and the Town's parks follow the water. LaVern M. Johnson Park, under the cliffs off Broadway, has the whitewater park, a splash pad, playgrounds, the campground and, in a cold January, an outdoor ice rink. Bohn Park, across the river from downtown on 2nd Avenue, has the ball fields, a dog park, an accessible fishing area and the trailhead for the town's dirt-jump bike park. Sandstone Park, at 4th and Broadway, is the downtown green with the visitor center and the summer concert stage.

Tubing on the North St. Vrain is the town's summer sport: a quarter-mile of pools and drops through the whitewater park when the flow is right, usually July and August. In May and June the runoff is high and cold and not for tubes. Anglers work both forks; a Colorado fishing licence applies.

Parking
The park lots take a Town parking pass on busy days; non-residents can buy a day or season pass. Street parking downtown is free.

03

Trails

Four open-space areas ring the town, and none is more than fifteen minutes from Main Street. Hall Ranch, on Colorado 7 just west of town, is the Front Range's best-known mountain-bike climb and a fine hike; the Bitterbrush trail gains about 1,500 feet to a loop through ponderosa meadows. Heil Valley Ranch, south off US 36, connects to Hall Ranch by the Picture Rock trail and has its own loops. Rabbit Mountain, north-east off Colorado 66, is the low, dry, early-season option with views back to Longs Peak. Button Rock Preserve, up County Road 80 west of town, is a gentle walk along the North St. Vrain to Longmont's reservoir dam.

Hall Ranch, Heil Valley Ranch and Rabbit Mountain are Boulder County Parks & Open Space; Button Rock is the City of Longmont's. No dogs at Hall Ranch, Heil Valley Ranch or Button Rock; leashed dogs are welcome at Rabbit Mountain. The county posts closures after storms and during elk calving. Distances, climbs and parking for every trail are on the Trails & River page.

04

Public art and the Redstone Museum

The heARTS of Lyons is an outdoor collection of some thirty sculptures placed along Main Street, in the parks and at the town's edges. Pieces are chosen through an annual call and stay for two years, so the collection turns over; a few have been bought for the permanent collection. The Lyons Arts & Humanities Commission, a Town commission, curates it, and a self-guided walking tour is online.

The Lyons Redstone Museum is the 1881 town school, a two-storey block of rusticated sandstone on High Street, run by the Lyons Historical Society since 1979. Inside are the quarry days, the railroad, the floods and the families, plus a research library and a gift shop. Admission is free; it opens for the summer season.

05

Planet Bluegrass and the music

A town this size should not have a nationally known music scene, and yet. Planet Bluegrass, a 20-acre ranch on the river at the west end of Main, hosts RockyGrass in late July and the Rocky Mountain Folks Festival in August, both with on-site camping and both selling out. The rest of the year the music is in the taprooms: a Sunday serenade and a Tuesday pick at Oskar Blues, a Thursday pick at MainStage, an open mic at the Rock Garden, and touring acts on the weekends.
