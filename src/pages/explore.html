01

Main Street and the stone town

Main Street is US 36 for six blocks, running east–west between the two highway junctions, with the hogback closing the view at the west end.

The buildings are the point. Fifteen structures of locally quarried red sandstone, built between the 1870s and 1917, are listed together on the National Register — and most are still in everyday use as shops, kitchens and offices.

  • Twenty minutes to walk it end to end.
  • High Street, one block north, adds coffee, wine and the museum.
  • Accredited Main Street America program and state Creative District.
Getting there
Street parking along Main and the side streets, free; a Town lot at 4th and Broadway beside Sandstone Park.
Best for
Coffee, lunch, the galleries and gift shops, and the building fronts themselves. Pick up the walking-tour leaflet at the visitor center in Sandstone Park.

02

The river and the parks

The North and South St. Vrain come out of their canyons and join in the middle of town. The Town’s three parks follow the water.

LaVern M. Johnson
Under the cliffs off Broadway: the whitewater park, a splash pad, playgrounds, the campground, and an outdoor ice rink in a cold January.
Bohn Park
Across the river on 2nd Avenue: ball fields, a dog park, accessible fishing, and the way in to the dirt-jump bike park.
Sandstone Park
4th and Broadway: the downtown green, the visitor center and the summer concert stage.

Tubing the North St. Vrain is the summer sport — a quarter-mile of pools and drops when the flow is right, usually July and August. May and June runoff is high, cold and not for tubes. Anglers work both forks with a Colorado licence.

Parking
The park lots take a Town parking pass on busy days; non-residents can buy a day or season pass. Street parking downtown is free.

03

Trails

Four open-space areas ring the town. None is more than fifteen minutes from Main Street.

Hall Ranch
The Front Range’s best-known bike climb, and a fine hike. Bitterbrush gains about 1,500 feet to a loop through ponderosa meadows.
Trailhead on CO 7, a mile west of town. Bikes yes, dogs no. County page ↗
Heil Valley Ranch
Linked to Hall Ranch by the Picture Rock trail, with loops of its own.
Picture Rock trailhead on Red Gulch Road, south of town off US 36. No dogs. County page ↗
Rabbit Mountain
Low, dry and the first to melt out, with the view back to Longs Peak.
Off N. 53rd Street from CO 66. Leashed dogs welcome. County page ↗
Button Rock
A gentle walk along the North St. Vrain to Longmont’s reservoir dam.
End of County Road 80, west of town. Foot travel only, no dogs. City page ↗
Closures
The county posts closures after storms and during elk calving. County trail closures and alerts ↗

04

Public art and the Redstone Museum

The heARTS of Lyons is an outdoor collection of some thirty sculptures, placed along Main Street, in the parks and at the town’s edges.

Pieces are chosen by annual call and stay two years, so the collection turns over; a few have been bought outright. The Town’s Arts & Humanities Commission curates it, and the walking tour is online.

The Lyons Redstone Museum is the 1881 town school on High Street, run by the Lyons Historical Society since 1979.

Inside: the quarry days, the railroad, the floods and the families, plus a research library and a gift shop. Admission is free, for the summer season.

05

Planet Bluegrass and the music

A town this size should not have a nationally known music scene. And yet.

Planet Bluegrass is a 20-acre ranch on the river at the west end of Main. It hosts RockyGrass in late July and the Rocky Mountain Folks Festival in August — both with on-site camping, both selling out.

The rest of the year the music is in the taprooms: a Sunday serenade and Tuesday pick at Oskar Blues, a Thursday pick at MainStage, an open mic at the Rock Garden, and touring acts at weekends.
