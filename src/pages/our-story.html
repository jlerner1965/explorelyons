Lyons History: Sandstone, the Railroad & the River

A quarry town at the mouth of two canyons, built of the rock it dug, cut off by its river once a century, and rebuilt each time by the people who live here.

Lyons exists because of a rock.

The Lyons Formation is a band of Permian sandstone running along the Front Range. Here it breaks into flat, even, red-pink slabs that split cleanly and barely weather. Edward S. Lyon saw it in 1880, bought 160 acres at the confluence of the North and South St. Vrain, and platted a town to sell it.

That stone is Main Street. It is also the University of Colorado campus in Boulder, and flagstone patios across the country.

What the quarries started, the canyons kept going. Lyons is the last town before the mountains on two highways, and for a century it has lived on people heading to Estes Park — and, lately, on people stopping. The river that made the town site has twice tried to take it back.

Timeline

How the town got here

Every entry names its source and links to it. Where a date is contested, it says so rather than guessing.

  1. Before 1880
    Lyons Redstone Museum ↗

    The confluence, before the town

    The St. Vrain valleys were Arapaho and Cheyenne country, with Ute bands crossing from the mountains. The Fort Laramie Treaty of 1851 acknowledged the land between the Platte and the Arkansas as theirs.

    The creeks took their name from Ceran St. Vrain’s trading post on the plains in the 1830s. Ranchers and gold-seekers came up the canyons after 1859, but no town stood at the confluence until the sandstone was noticed.

  2. 1880–1881
    Wikipedia, Lyons, Colorado ↗

    Edward Lyon buys the rock

    Edward S. Lyon, from Connecticut, bought 160 acres at the confluence in 1880 and platted the town in 1881, naming it for himself. The quarries opened on the hogbacks around it and the school, a two-storey block of the same stone, went up the same year. That schoolhouse is the Redstone Museum today.

  3. 1884–1885
    Town of Lyons, the Depot ↗

    The railroad and the depot

    The Denver, Utah & Pacific reached the quarries in 1884, and the stone depot on Broadway opened in 1885, later a Burlington line. Sandstone left by the trainload for Denver, Boulder and the Midwest, and the town filled with quarrymen — many of them Welsh, Cornish, Swedish and Italian.

    The depot worked until the 1960s, joined the National Register in 1974, and was the town library from 1984 until the 2013 flood.

  4. 1891
    Statutory town, Colorado

    Incorporation

    Lyons incorporated as a statutory town in 1891 with a mayor and board of trustees — the form it still keeps. The Old Stone Congregational Church of 1894 and a run of Main Street commercial blocks date from the boom that followed.

    Fifteen sandstone buildings from the 1870s to 1917 are listed together on the National Register of Historic Places.

  6. 1910s–1930s
    Wikipedia, Lyons, Colorado ↗

    Cement kills the quarries, and flagstone revives them

    Portland cement undercut building stone around 1910, and the big quarries closed.

    The rock came back in the 1930s as decorative flagstone and veneer. It is still cut in the hills around town, on a smaller scale, and shipped worldwide. Meanwhile the town settled into its other trade: the last stop before Estes Park.

  7. 1973–1979
    Lyons Redstone Museum ↗

    The Historical Society, the depot and the museum

    Residents formed the Lyons Historical Society in 1973 to stop the depot being sold and trucked out of town. They bought it with the Town and restored it, and in 1979 opened the Redstone Museum in the old school.

    RockyGrass — the bluegrass festival that would settle at Planet Bluegrass on the west edge of town — dates from the same decade.

  8. 1997–2010
    Colorado Main Street spotlight ↗

    Beer, bluegrass and a downtown award

    Oskar Blues opened as a Cajun restaurant on Main Street in 1997. In 2002 it became the first American craft brewery to can its own beer — a decision that changed the industry and carried the town’s name well beyond Colorado.

    The Arts & Humanities Commission began placing sculpture around town, the annual studio tour started in 2004, and in 2010 the downtown won the Governor’s Award for Downtown Excellence.

  9. September 2013
    Wikipedia, 2013 Colorado floods ↗

    The flood

    After a week of rain, both forks of the St. Vrain rose on the night of September 11–12, 2013 and met in the middle of town. Every road in was cut, the water, sewer and power went, and the National Guard evacuated residents by convoy and helicopter.

    About a fifth of the housing was lost or badly damaged. The parks and the depot library were wrecked. Rebuilding the utilities, bridges and riverbanks took years of federal, state and Town money.

    LaVern M. Johnson Park reopened in 2017; the library moved into a new building in 2019.

  10. Today
    2020 census; Town of Lyons

    A stone town at the canyon mouth

    The 2020 census counted 2,209 people in 1.36 square miles. The downtown is an accredited Main Street America program and a state Creative District, and about 97% of its businesses are independently owned.

    Two festivals, two breweries, a distillery, a tiny-home resort and a mountain-bike trail keep the through-traffic stopping. The quarries on the hogbacks are still working.
