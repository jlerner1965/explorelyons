What is Lyons, Colorado?

Lyons is a statutory town of about 2,200 people in northwestern Boulder County, at the mouth of the St. Vrain canyons where US 36 and Colorado 7 leave the plains for the mountains. It was platted in 1881 around red sandstone quarries and incorporated in 1891.

Read the town’s history →

Where is Lyons?

About 17 miles north of Boulder on US 36, 12 miles west of Longmont on Colorado 66, and 20 miles east of Estes Park. Both highways to Rocky Mountain National Park pass through it, which is why the town calls itself the Double Gateway to the Rockies.

Get directions and parking details →

What can you do in Lyons?

Walk Main Street’s stone storefronts and galleries, tube or kayak the North St. Vrain, ride or hike Hall Ranch and Heil Valley Ranch, tour the sculpture collection, visit the Redstone Museum, and end at a brewery or the distillery.

Where can you eat and shop?

Nearly everything is on Main Street between the two highway junctions, with a second small cluster on High Street a block north. About 97% of the town’s businesses are independently owned; the directory groups them by category and links to each for current hours.

Browse restaurants and shops →

What events happen in Lyons?

RockyGrass and the Folks Festival at Planet Bluegrass each summer, free concerts in Sandstone Park, artisan markets, the Outdoor Games, Old Man Winter, a Halloween parade and the Parade of Lights, plus live music somewhere in town most nights of the week.

Check upcoming events →

Who runs the town?

An elected mayor and six trustees, meeting the first and third Mondays. The Town runs its own water, sewer and electric utilities, the parks and the campgrounds; the Boulder County Sheriff polices it and a fire protection district covers fire and EMS.
